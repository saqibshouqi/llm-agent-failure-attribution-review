01 / The research problem

When an AI agent fails partway through a long task, should it start over or can it safely recover?

Long-horizon LLM agents interact with tools, memory, and external environments. A failure in one step can propagate through subsequent actions, making it difficult to identify the true cause and safely preserve earlier work.

This systematic review synthesizes 68 studies and examines how failure detection, causal localization, rollback, state and context restoration, repair, and evaluation could work together as a coherent recovery process.

Central finding

Among the 60 primary studies reviewed, only two integrated localization, rollback/checkpointing, and repair/recovery under the study's coding criteria. None covered the complete recovery chain together with context handling, cost, and evaluation.

02 / Main contributions

01

Evidence map & research gaps

A systematic synthesis of 68 studies, with an analysis of how existing approaches connect failure localization, rollback, repair, context management, cost, and evaluation.

02

Six-dimensional taxonomy

A classification spanning failure type, localization granularity, localization evidence, recovery strategy, state reversibility, and evaluation criteria.

03

Recovery decision framework

A conceptual, reversibility-aware recovery loop and Break-Even Attribution Accuracy (BAA) construct to examine when targeted recovery may outperform safe restart.

03 / Proposed recovery loop

The review proposes linking four reliability stages rather than treating error detection, attribution, and recovery as isolated mechanisms.

01→

Observe

Detect failed, invalid, or unsafe agent trajectories.

02→

Diagnose

Localize the cause and select a safe recovery boundary.

03→

Recover

Restore state and context, then repair or compensate as appropriate.

04✓

Validate

Re-execute and measure rescue success, cost, consistency, and safety.

This is a conceptual research framework and evaluation agenda, not an empirically validated recovery system.
